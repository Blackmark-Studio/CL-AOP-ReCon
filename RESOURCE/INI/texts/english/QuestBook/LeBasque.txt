Hunting_huntsman {
Hunting the Hunters
}
Hunting_huntsman.0 {
It is time to send Don Diego Vargas to the next world. The story of this bloodhound is instructive... Once a brilliant officer of the Spanish Armada, he lost the royal treasury during a filibuster attack. Condemned and disgraced, Vargas offered his services to the Crown. Now he ruthlessly hunts the boucanier brotherhood, hoping to restore his good name and win the governorship of Maracaibo.

Le Basque intends to dismantle his enemy methodically. Vargas's secretary and treasurer, Esteban Molina, is in Havana; all the money and secret dispatches pass through him. Le Basque's agent, Gaspar Leroux, has also been there for two weeks, posing as a Marseille wine merchant and sniffing out information. I must find Leroux, get to Molina, and take the treasurer alive. Havana it is.
}
Hunting_huntsman.1 {
Gaspar Leroux brought bad news in Havana... The treasurer fled yesterday aboard the 'La Paloma', bound for Santa Catalina. He has a casket containing all of Vargas's secrets. My task is to catch the ship, take Molina alive, and seize the papers without sending the brig to the bottom too soon. Time to make sail.
}
Hunting_huntsman.2 {
Molina proved quicker than I expected. The 'La Paloma' managed to anchor at Santa Catalina, and now Molina is safely hidden behind the walls of a Spanish fort. Looking for him in that hornet's nest would be suicide; the fish has slipped away with all of Vargas's secrets. Returning to le Basque empty-handed would be as good as signing my own death warrant...
}
Hunting_huntsman.3 {
I caught the fugitive, but in the heat of battle the brig went down with Molina and all of Vargas's secrets. Le Basque made himself perfectly clear: a dead treasurer and drowned papers are not worth a broken piastre. Returning to him with such news would be a fine way to earn a bullet. I will have to forget this whole affair...
}
Hunting_huntsman.4 {
The 'La Paloma' has been boarded. Molina tried to threaten me with his connections, but turned pale at the mere mention of le Basque and meekly went down to the hold. I can return to Tortuga.
}
Hunting_huntsman.5 {
The prisoner and his papers are now in le Basque's hands. Yet the Spaniard is not Michel's only concern: Jaco went into the jungle this morning and still has not returned. Michel says that for that little fox to get lost is like a fish drowning. If he is right, I had better hurry. There may still be tracks to find in the jungle.
}
Hunting_huntsman.6 {
Jaco turned up in a cave. Vargas's men were trying to beat information out of him, but I reached them in time. The lad brought excellent news: Don Diego sensed he was being watched in Havana, went to ground, and sent his lieutenants across the archipelago to prepare for war. Under interrogation Molina gave them all away, and le Basque has decided to cut off Vargas's hands.
Ignacio Vela is hiding in San Juan. He is cautious, so he will have to be removed quietly.
Sebastian Rojas has dug in at a camp in the Jamaican jungle; that one will require an assault.
And Martin Quesada is hiding near Bermuda and never comes ashore; we will take him at sea.
Le Basque has begun involving Jaco in work of this kind... The youngster has come aboard my ship.
Tomorrow I need to call on le Basque. Etienne Marceau will be there and will sail with me aboard his own ship. In the meantime, I should find old Rimalie in the jungle... He wanted to speak with me.
}
Hunting_huntsman.6.1 {
Old boucanier Nicolas Rimalie has finally choked on the boredom of life ashore and insistently asked to join my ship as an officer. I had to grant the veteran's request and take him into my service. Let swordplay remain the province of younger men; the keen eye and heavy musket of this seasoned hunter will serve me well.
}
Hunting_huntsman.6.2 {
Old hunter Nicolas Rimalie was desperate to join my crew, eager to trade a safe old age for powder smoke. For all my respect for his past deeds, I had to refuse him. Better he spend his remaining years ashore over a mug of strong rum. This time he will come along to keep an eye on Jaco, but afterward he will go ashore with him.

Hunting_huntsman.6.3 {
Etienne Marceau has joined my squadron. His experience and cool calculation will be a fine addition to the coming hunt for Vargas's men.

Hunting_huntsman.7 {
Quesada abandoned his refuge in Bermuda in such haste that he left one of his own officers behind. Resentment loosened the Spaniard's tongue: Don Martin sailed for the Turks, where two supply ships are waiting for him.

Hunting_huntsman.8 {
The support ships Vargas sent did the Spaniard no good. Quesada is dead. Don Diego has lost his best sea hound.

Hunting_huntsman.9 {
We have anchored off Hispaniola in Fajardo Bay. We cannot enter the port openly. Marceau's plan is this: we split up. Young Jaco goes into town to track down Don Vela's contact, while I head to the tavern. There I must find a certain Bartolomeo and persuade him to leave the door of the port office unlocked.

I met Bartolomeo. According to him, the port office door will be left open tomorrow after midnight. I need to slip inside and steal a blank Castilian form.

The lad handled it sensibly: to keep the prisoner's screams from drawing a patrol, he dragged him to Fajardo Beach. I will have to take a walk to the shore myself. Let us see how this fanatic sings once I get my hands on him.

I finally shook the necessary information out of the stubborn bastard. A few gentle words and the prospect of being eaten alive by wild crabs loosened his tongue quickly enough. Michel le Basque knows how to reward an ally. Fifty thousand ringing piastres is a worthy price for the heads of Vargas's three lieutenants. The silver weighs pleasantly in my purse, while our chief enemy has crawled into a hole to lick his wounds.

Brides_Tortuga {
The Rose of the Winds
}
Brides_Tortuga.0 {
Le Basque has finally shown his hand. His true target is Maracaibo. The city's impregnability is almost the stuff of legend. But the more secure people feel, the quicker they lose their vigilance. The venture is risky, yet the prize promises to be so rich that even the most cautious man would think twice before refusing. Before challenging the Spaniards on the Main, we must deal with a threat on our own doorstep. It seems Vargas has struck from the shadows again and intercepted the French flute 'Rose of the Winds'. Michel has asked me to speak with Governor d'Ogeron and learn everything the residence knows about the ship's disappearance.
}
Brides_Tortuga.1 {
Governor d'Ogeron confirmed the worst: the flute 'Rose of the Winds' vanished without a trace on its approach to Tortuga. She carried settlers without whom the plans to develop the outpost are in jeopardy. The governor did not say a word about Urbain Bellefleur, but that can hardly be an accident; such matters are not discussed with outsiders. Henri Tessier reported that the patrols found neither wreckage nor signs of battle. So the flute either strayed off course or someone went to great lengths to make her disappearance go unnoticed. The last lead is a fish merchant named Laurent. Tessier is certain he may know something the patrols missed.
}
Brides_Tortuga.2 {
Old Laurent did not disappoint. His smuggler friends nearly collided with the 'Rose of the Winds' in open water. With all lights extinguished, the flute passed along the coast and headed for the northern side of the island. No sooner had I learned this than a harbor boy handed me a note. An unknown writer claims there is no point looking for the flute now and proposes a meeting at midnight in the cemetery. A curious place for a conversation. We shall see who comes to this rendezvous.
}
Brides_Tortuga.3 {
The cemetery meeting explained much. Vargas did not merely intercept the 'Rose of the Winds'; he had penetrated deep into Tortuga's garrison and bribed part of the guard. It seems d'Ogeron had long suspected treachery and therefore sought support from the Order of Malta, so that when the time came he could purge the garrison and temporarily entrust the town's defense to men he could trust.
The settlers are being held in the catacombs beneath Tortuga under guard by corrupt French soldiers. Marie Bellefleur is not among them: Vargas took her with him and left with his most loyal men. The captain of the guard remained underground and surely knows where they went. I need him alive.
}
Brides_Tortuga.4 {
The traitors received their due: a journey to Satan's realm. The prisoners have gone to the governor. Urbain Bellefleur, however, was not among them. I assume he either did not survive the capture of the ship or was taken elsewhere. Vargas has not had time to get far: he took Marie to a schooner anchored off Fish Eye Beach, waiting for the tide. Rimalie proposed a bold plan: use a secret passage to reach the shore, swim to the schooner, and silently kill the watch. One thing I do not understand is why Vargas bothered dragging the prisoners into the catacombs.
}
Brides_Tortuga.5 {
The schooner off Fish Eye Beach was bait. Vargas hired her crew to send us down a false trail, then ordered everyone who could tell the tale killed. The captain lived just long enough to point us in the right direction: before the massacre, Vargas had been asking about Chetumal Bay near Belize, took a navigator familiar with those waters, and headed there. Marie was not aboard the schooner, which means Vargas still has her.
Rimalie is certain Vargas deliberately left us a trail and expects us to rush after him. I think otherwise: he tried to make his flight look like a clever trap, but in truth he simply ran with his tail between his legs. I will not let him escape. Not now, when he is this weak.
}
Brides_Tortuga.6 {
Rimalie was right after all... Vargas meant to lure le Basque into a trap, but Nicolas and I ended up in it instead. Had Marceau not arrived in time, that mine would have become our final resting place.
Still, we outplayed Vargas. His corvette is at the bottom, and he has no road left to retreat. All that remains is to find the well and finally repay what I owe him. More importantly, I must tear Marie Bellefleur from his claws.
}
Brides_Tortuga.7 {
Damn it... Vargas's blade was smeared with some filth. I was unconscious for only a few seconds, but that was enough for him to slip away. Marie is alive, though still unconscious. Damn him... I hate leaving her here alone, but I cannot let that bastard escape. I need to catch Vargas and end this here and now.
}
Brides_Tortuga.8 {
Vargas's poison left me unconscious for two days. Marceau and Rimalie took care of both Marie and me, as well as the detachments entrenched in the jungle. I feel as though I have been dragged beneath a keel, but Madame Bellefleur seems far worse off. When the 'Rose of the Winds' was seized, the Spaniards killed nearly every man aboard. Her uncle was among the dead. It is a terrible blow for Marie. I should speak with her once I am properly on my feet.
For now the plan is simple: set course for Tortuga. Michel will surely want to hear how our hunt ended.
}
Brides_Tortuga.9 {
Michel is pleased with how the whole affair ended. No wonder: Vargas was defeated, Marie's papers were delivered, Tortuga was saved, and the settlement gained a number of new residents who seem determined to celebrate their arrival properly.
D'Ogeron sent his thanks for destroying the corrupt soldiers and rescuing the settlers, and also gave them permission to settle under Michel's protection.
I received fifty thousand for the whole business. Le Basque told me to spend at least part of it on rest and recreation. How could I refuse? Especially since tomorrow will offer a perfect opportunity.
}

Keys to the Lagoon

Keys_lagoon.14 {
The Spaniards left in the governor's bedroom were hurriedly burning documents. We burst in just in time to snatch a surviving dispatch from the flames. Now it is clear where the townsfolk and soldiers went: the treasury, jewels, and goods were sent overland to Gibraltar. We must find Michel at once. If we hurry, we may still catch the caravan.
}
Keys_lagoon.15 {
The last copper-bound chest has safely disappeared into the depths of the hold, bringing our exhausting labors to an end. Over the past few days, sweating beneath the blazing Caribbean sun, the corsairs transferred the entire heavy treasury of Gibraltar and the silver recovered from the Spaniards at Maracaibo onto four vessels: those of le Basque, Montbars, Francois l'Olonnais, and Marceau. Their ships, riding low in the water, have already passed the mouth of the lagoon, spread their sails, and vanished into the sea haze on a course for Tortuga. Now it is my turn to weigh anchor. There is no sense lingering in these plundered, hostile waters, and Michel strongly advised against tempting fate with needless delays. The final division of our fabulous spoils will take place behind closed doors on Tortuga once the entire squadron has anchored again in its home harbor, bringing a fitting end to this extraordinary campaign.
}
Keys_lagoon.16 {
So ends our extraordinary expedition, which thundered across the entire Spanish Main. Heavy iron-bound chests containing Vargas's treasury and the silver taken back at Maracaibo were opened behind the closed doors of the mansion on Tortuga. The spoils were divided fairly, and every captain received his lawful share, richly paid for in blood. The great alliance of fearsome corsairs that made the Castilians tremble dissolved as swiftly as it had arisen. The indomitable Francois l'Olonnais, the cold-blooded Daniel Montbars, the elusive Marie, and old Rimalie all went their separate ways, some in search of new prizes, others in pursuit of noisy revelry and long-awaited rest. Before we parted, Michel le Basque, whose old personal vendetta had been the true cause of this grand expedition, gave me his unbreakable word of honor. From now on I can count on his help, and this is no mere courtesy, but an oath that will stand until one of us goes to feed the fish. The campaign can be considered a brilliant success. My holds are heavy with Castilian gold, and my name is forever written into the bloody and glorious history of the Caribbean Sea. Time to set sail toward new adventures!
}
Keys_lagoon.17 {
Spain's final debt has been paid in blood. The punitive squadron sent on the orders of Don Alonso de Vargas finally caught us in open water. Two frigates, a heavy galleon, and a corvette went to the bottom with their crews. The accounts are settled for good. The Maracaibo expedition is over.
}
